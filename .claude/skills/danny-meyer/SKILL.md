# Danny Meyer Hospitality Logic

## Core Philosophy: Enlightened Hospitality

From "Setting the Table" (Danny Meyer, 2006):

> "The most successful businesses are those where the staff is so well taken care of that they take great care of their guests."

**Priority Hierarchy (in order):**

1. Employees
2. Guests
3. Community
4. Suppliers
5. Investors

This is counterintuitive. Most operators put guests first. Meyer's insight: happy staff creates happy guests, which creates profit, which makes investors happy.

## The 51% Rule

When hiring, 51% of the evaluation is emotional intelligence (EQ):

- Warmth and optimism
- Intelligence about people
- Work ethic (and evidence of it)
- Empathy, self-awareness
- Integrity

The remaining 49% is technical skill. **You can teach technique. You cannot teach caring.**

## The Virtuous Cycle

```
Hire for EQ + train for skill
    ↓
Staff feels safe + valued
    ↓
Staff extends genuine hospitality
    ↓
Guests feel seen, not just served
    ↓
Guests return + refer others
    ↓
Revenue → reinvest in staff
    ↓
(repeat)
```

## The 4 Components of Genuine Hospitality

1. **Making guests feel welcome** — did they feel they arrived in a place that wanted them?
2. **Making guests feel honored** — did we show them we were glad they were here?
3. **Making guests feel important** — did we make them feel their presence mattered?
4. **Making guests feel at home** — did we create genuine ease and comfort?

## Errors of Omission vs Commission

Danny Meyer's hardest lesson:

- **Error of commission**: You did something wrong (wrong dish, wrong price)
- **Error of omission**: You failed to do something right (forgot the birthday candle, didn't notice they were cold)

**Errors of omission are invisible until the guest never comes back.**
Nanny's event runbook should be built to prevent errors of omission with explicit checkpoints.

## The "51% Club" Prompt for Hiring

When evaluating kitchen or service staff, ask:

1. Tell me about a time you made someone feel genuinely welcomed — not just served.
2. What's something you do at work that no one would notice if you stopped doing it, but you do it anyway?
3. How do you handle a guest who's wrong but upset?

Score EQ first. Technical skill second.

## Revenue Insight

Meyer's USHG (Union Square Hospitality Group) famously eliminated tipping and moved to "Hospitality Included" pricing — building service cost into menu prices. This:

- Reduced front/back wage inequality
- Improved team morale
- Aligned incentives (quality over speed)

For Nanny users: Price your service fee into the menu. Don't charge a "service gratuity" separately — build it in and explain what it funds.
